Overdispersion lives in daily totals, not hours

Hourly: Poisson is enough

Pearson χ²/df 1.10 · Cameron–Trivedi α = −0.057 (t = −7.6) · NB2 α̂ on the boundary, 95% bound 0.0004

Daily: NB2 is needed

Pearson 1.36 · α̂ = 0.0168 ± 0.0017, LR = 142 · 90% coverage 87.9% vs 84.3% for Poisson

Why: day-level shocks (weather, events) add up across hours, while busy hours are capped by free ports.

Patil · Bera · Tyagi — EV charging demand, Palo Alto 2017–2019

5

This was our most surprising finding, and we report it honestly instead of forcing the usual "use NB because of overdispersion" story. Cameron-Trivedi: regress ((y - mu)^2 - y)/mu on mu without intercept; a positive slope means NB2-type overdispersion. Hourly it is negative (slight underdispersion); in the busiest decile variance/mean falls to 0.89 because arrivals are limited by free ports. So at hourly resolution M3 collapses to M2 (alpha-hat = 0, LR = 0). At daily resolution the variance grows like mu + 0.0168 mu^2, and NB2 gives better-calibrated intervals; its PIT tail share is 14.4% vs 18.6% for Poisson (nominal 10%). Planners think in days, so NB2 is the right model for planning-scale uncertainty.
