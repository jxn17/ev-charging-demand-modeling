The figure is Fig. 8 of the paper. Left panel: every dot is one site-hour; points on the diagonal mean the simulator reproduces real occupancy. Right panel: where drivers would be turned away, mostly Hamilton and Webster in the morning. The scenario numbers on the cards are Table X. Each site is an M_t/G/c/c loss system on a typical 2019 weekday: non-homogeneous Poisson arrivals by Lewis-Shedler thinning, plug-in durations resampled from that site's 2019 weekday sessions that started in the same hour, c = active ports, a driver who finds all ports busy leaves. 500 replications, fixed seed, one warm-up day. Validation: with observed arrival rates the simulated occupancy matches the occupancy rebuilt from start + duration (r 0.988-0.999); with M3 rates the shape matches but the level carries the 9.4% forecast shortfall. Erlang-B at the peak arrival hour overstates the simulated blocking at 7 of 8 sites, because occupancy lags arrivals. Overflow = try your own garage, then the new site; pooled = any free port in the cluster. Figures are per year on 251 non-holiday weekdays, and are lower bounds because the log never records drivers who were turned away.
